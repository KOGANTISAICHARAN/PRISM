PRISM
 AI-Powered Food Safety Early-Warning Platform
 Complete Ideathon Master Guide
One document for the entire team: problem, users, solution, features, AI, food inspector workflow,
Evidence Vault, risk engine, clustering, hotspot detection, architecture, database, MVP,
implementation plan, demo, business model, safety, and judge questions.
Core idea: PRISM connects citizen reports, AI-assisted analysis, evidence integrity, risk scoring,
complaint clustering and food-inspector review into one continuous loop.
Team rule: Build one convincing end-to-end journey instead of many disconnected features.

1. Executive Summary
PRISM is a citizen-powered food-safety early-warning platform. A citizen can report a possible food-safety issue using a
photo and simple description. PRISM uses AI to analyze visible signals and structure the complaint, stores supporting
evidence in an integrity-aware evidence record, calculates risk signals, groups similar complaints, and highlights potential
hotspots to a food inspector or authorized reviewer.
The inspector then reviews the evidence, investigates the case, adds inspection evidence, records findings and updates
the case status. PRISM therefore creates a feedback loop from citizen → intelligence → human investigation → citizen
status.
Important positioning: PRISM is a decision-support and reporting platform. AI does not prove
contamination, declare a business guilty, or replace official food-safety procedures.
2. The Problem
Food-safety problems are often discovered after a customer notices an issue, complains, or becomes ill. Ordinary citizens
may find reporting difficult, may not know which information is useful, and may have little visibility into what happens after
submission.
At the same time, individual complaints can remain fragmented. A single report may look minor, while many similar
reports from the same vendor or area can indicate an emerging pattern.
Core problem statement: How can we make food-safety reporting simple for ordinary citizens and
convert scattered reports into early-warning signals that help authorized food-safety teams
prioritize investigation sooner?
3. Why Existing Processes Leave a Gap
The technical blueprint supplied with this project describes three commonly separate tracks: a citizen complaint portal, an
evidence model that may simply attach files to a ticket, and backend risk-scoring systems that can operate separately
from the citizen reporting experience.
Its central observation is that PRISM attempts to fuse these tracks into one loop: the same citizen action can open a case,
contribute to evidence, update risk signals, and feed complaint clustering and hotspot detection.
PRISM's opportunity: connect the citizen's real-world narrative and evidence with the intelligence layer used to prioritize
human review.

11. Complaint Clustering
PRISM groups reports that may describe the same or related issue. The prototype can use semantic text embeddings
plus additional metadata such as vendor, time and location.
Example:
 “Bad smell from chicken”
 “Chicken tasted spoiled”
 “Food had a strange smell”
 “Felt unwell after eating there”
If other signals align, the system can create a possible incident cluster instead of treating the reports as unrelated tickets.
Output: “Possible incident cluster — 14 related reports.”

16. Inspector Investigation Mode
 Visit establishment
 Verify relevant licence/details according to applicable process
 Inspect kitchen hygiene
 Check storage conditions
 Check food handling
 Record observations
 Upload appropriate inspection evidence
 Record findings and next step
The exact regulatory procedure depends on the competent authority. PRISM should support the workflow rather than
invent regulatory powers.
17. Inspector Evidence Upload
The inspector can add new evidence such as inspection photographs, documents and notes. New evidence should be
created as a new immutable record rather than modifying an existing evidence row.
The same evidence-integrity principles apply: SHA-256 hash, uploader, timestamp, location where available, and audit
events.

Why this matters: the evidence chain continues from citizen submission to inspector investigation instead of splitting the
case into unrelated systems.
18. Human Verification & Decision
 AI signal → Inspector review → Investigation → Human decision
PRISM can recommend that a case deserves attention. It must not automatically declare contamination, guilt, or
enforcement action.
If a citizen describes illness symptoms, the assistant should encourage appropriate medical attention and continue
collecting the report separately; it must not diagnose the user.
19. Citizen Feedback Loop
After the authorized reviewer updates the case, the citizen sees a simple status timeline.
Report #PR-10482
✓ Submitted
✓ Evidence received
✓ Under investigation

This feedback loop is important because the citizen is not just a data source; they are a user who should understand what
happened to their report.

25. Evidence Vault Technical Rules
 Hash every uploaded file with SHA-256 server-side before storage processing.
 Do not edit an evidence row after creation; corrections create a new row.
 Use object storage with versioning where possible.
 Maintain an append-only audit log for CREATE, VIEW, EXPORT and DOWNLOAD.
 Store uploader, GPS and upload timestamp.
 Retain capture timestamp from EXIF when available.
 Restrict application-layer deletion rights.
 Use access control based on user role and case jurisdiction.

26. Complaint Assistant Technical Rules
 Use guided slot filling, not open-ended autonomous conversation.
 Run image analysis before asking questions.
 Never re-ask fields already known.
 Ask at most one or two short questions per turn.
 Use possible/suspected/reported language.
 Never diagnose illness.
 When all fields are filled, emit structured report data for Review & Submit.
 Keep the final citizen confirmation step explicit.

 Do not publicly label businesses unsafe from unverified reports.

37. Judge Questions & Strong Answers
Q: Why can't users simply use existing food-safety complaint channels?
A: They can. PRISM is not trying to replace official channels. It reduces reporting friction, structures evidence and adds
pattern/risk intelligence so multiple reports can become useful early-warning signals.
Q: What is innovative?
A: The integrated loop: AI-assisted intake, evidence integrity, semantic complaint clustering, dynamic risk signals, hotspot
detection and human inspector review in one system.
Q: Can AI prove contamination from an image?
A: No. PRISM only identifies possible visible issues. Confirmation requires appropriate human investigation and, where
applicable, laboratory or official processes.
Q: Does AI decide whether a restaurant is guilty?
A: No. AI produces signals and prioritization. The food inspector or authorized authority performs human review and
decides the appropriate action.
Q: Why is the Evidence Vault needed?
A: It makes evidence more structured and auditable than a simple attachment. Hashes help detect file changes, and audit
logs record access events.
Q: Why does the inspector need PRISM?
A: It brings reports, evidence, clusters, risk factors and hotspots into one prioritized workspace.
Q: What if the AI is wrong?
A: The result is explicitly treated as a possible signal. The inspector can review and reject it. Original evidence remains
available.
Q: Where does the data come from?

A: The ideathon prototype can use clearly labeled synthetic/demo data. A production deployment would need appropriate
official integrations, data-sharing arrangements and privacy controls.
Q: How do you monetize?
A: Keep core citizen reporting free and offer paid analytics/monitoring to restaurants, food chains and appropriate
institutional customers.
Q: Why should people use it if official complaint systems already exist?
A: PRISM can make the reporting experience easier while adding a layer that existing complaint tickets generally do not
provide: cross-report pattern detection and risk prioritization.

40. Final Project Statement

PRISM is an AI-powered food-safety early-warning platform that helps citizens report
potential food-safety issues, organizes and protects evidence, detects related complaint
patterns, calculates risk signals, identifies emerging hotspots, and gives food inspectors a
prioritized workspace for human investigation.
PRISM's philosophy is simple: one complaint is information; a pattern of complaints can
become an early warning.
41. Final Pitch
 “Today, a food-safety problem may be reported one complaint at a time. PRISM connects
those small signals. A citizen takes a photo, AI helps structure the report, evidence is
preserved, similar complaints are clustered, risk is assessed, and emerging hotspots are
highlighted for human review. We are not replacing food-safety authorities—we are giving
them an earlier and clearer signal of where to look.”
